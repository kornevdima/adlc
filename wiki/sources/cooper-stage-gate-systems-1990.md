# Stage-gate systems (Cooper, 1990)

Cooper's paper (Business Horizons 33(3), 44-54) defines the gate as a decision with criteria and evidence, not a document. The text was read through a third-party markdown summary of the paper, not the original, so confidence is medium. The paper is over 3 years old and foundational, so treat details of stage counts as potentially stale; the gate anatomy is still the common reference.

## Gate anatomy

- Deliverables: defined outputs of the prior stage that the team brings (a market analysis, a technical assessment, a business case). These are stage products, owned by the stage.
- Criteria: the questions and metrics used to judge, with mandatory (must-meet) and desirable (should-meet) items such as strategic fit, feasibility, return and resources.
- Output: a decision of go, kill, hold or recycle, with resources committed only on approval.

## What record a gate leaves

The summary reports no formal record beyond the decision itself; minutes or sign-off forms are not part of Cooper's description. A decision line plus the criteria it was judged against is enough to satisfy the model. (Medium confidence: absence in a summary is weak evidence.)
