# Business Case Anatomy and Financial Model Reference

## Part 1 — Section-by-Section Writing Guide

### Executive Summary
**Length:** One page maximum (approximately 400 words)
**Audience:** The most senior stakeholder who will read the document. Write for a person who
will read only this section.
**Must contain:**
- The problem or opportunity in two sentences
- The recommended option in one sentence
- The three most important financial figures (NPV, ROI, payback)
- The decision required and by when

**Must not contain:**
- Background context the reader already knows
- Technical jargon
- Detailed cost breakdowns or assumptions
- Hedged language ("it is possible that", "one could argue")

**Test:** If a reader makes the right decision after reading only the executive summary, it is correct.

---

### Problem Statement
**Purpose:** Establish the case for acting at all. If this section does not compel action, the
rest of the document cannot save it.

**Must contain:**
- Current state: factual description of how things work today
- Consequence of inaction: what gets worse, what risk materialises, what cost persists
- Affected stakeholders: who bears the consequence
- Evidence: at minimum one data point or reference (from gap analysis, industry benchmark, or observed metric)

**Must not contain:**
- The solution (save that for options)
- Speculation without a stated basis

---

### Strategic Alignment
**Purpose:** Connect the initiative to something the organisation has already committed to pursuing.
This section makes the initiative a strategic priority rather than a departmental request.

**Format:** Mapping table. Left column: initiative capability or outcome. Right column: matching
strategic objective, OKR, regulation, or programme.

**One sentence narrative per row:** "Delivering [initiative outcome] directly supports
[objective] by [mechanism]."

---

### Options Analysis
**Purpose:** Show that alternatives were considered and Do Nothing was costed.

**Structure per option:**
1. Option number and name
2. Description (what it involves in plain terms)
3. What is in scope and what is not
4. Key assumptions
5. Dependencies and prerequisites
6. High-level implementation timeline

**Do Nothing option must include:**
- Cost of ongoing problem (operational waste, error cost, compliance exposure, opportunity cost)
- Risk exposure if unaddressed
- Timeline to consequence (when does inaction become critical?)

---

### Financial Analysis
**Purpose:** Provide the quantitative basis for the recommendation.
**Tone:** Precise about assumptions, explicit about uncertainty. Every number has a source or a basis.

### Non-Financial Scoring
**Purpose:** Capture value that cannot be reduced to cash, and break ties between financially
close options.

**Do not omit this section** even if one option dominates financially. Non-financial criteria
often determine whether benefits are realisable at all (capability to deliver, stakeholder acceptance).

---

### Risk Assessment
**Purpose:** Show that risks are understood and have mitigations, not to scare the reader.

**Format:** Table per option. Three columns per risk: rating (L/M/H), description, mitigation.
**Keep to top 3–5 risks per option.** A long risk table signals poor scoping, not thoroughness.

---

### Recommendation
**The cardinal rule:** State the recommendation in the first sentence. Do not build up to it.
Do not say "based on the above analysis, it would appear that...". Say "Option 1 is recommended."

**Structure:**
1. Recommendation statement (one sentence)
2. Three financial reasons (NPV, payback, sensitivity passes)
3. Two non-financial reasons (strategic alignment score, lowest risk, etc.)
4. Primary risk to the recommendation and its mitigation
5. Conditions for success (what must be true for the recommended option to deliver its benefits)
6. Decision required: approve / reject / defer — from whom — by when
