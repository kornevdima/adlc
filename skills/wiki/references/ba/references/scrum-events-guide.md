# Scrum Events — Scrum Guide 2020 Reference

## Purpose
Use as the authoritative reference for all Scrum event definitions, purposes, time-boxes,
and artefact connections in ba-scrum-events-pack. All content is sourced from the
Scrum Guide 2020.

---

## The Sprint

**Definition:** The Sprint is the heartbeat of Scrum — a container for all other events.
Each Sprint may be considered a short project.

**Fixed properties:**
- Duration: one month or less; consistent duration throughout the development effort
- No changes are made that would endanger the Sprint Goal
- Quality does not decrease
- The Product Backlog is refined as needed
- Scope may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as more is learned

**Sprint cancellation:** Only the Product Owner may cancel a Sprint. A Sprint may be cancelled
if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete.

**BA implication:** Scope changes mid-Sprint go through the Product Owner. The BA does not
accept new requirements directly from stakeholders during a Sprint — they go to the Product
Backlog for future consideration.

---

## Sprint Planning

**Purpose:** Initiates the Sprint by laying out the work to be performed.
**Attendees:** The entire Scrum Team (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers)
**Time-box:** 8 hours maximum for a one-month Sprint. Shorter Sprints have proportionally shorter events.

**Three topics addressed:**
1. **Why is this Sprint valuable?** — The Product Owner proposes how the product could increase
   its value and utility. The Scrum Team collaborates to define a Sprint Goal.
2. **What can be Done this Sprint?** — Developers select items from the Product Backlog to include.
   Only Developers can assess what they can accomplish.
3. **How will the chosen work get done?** — Developers plan how to deliver the selected PBIs as
   an Increment meeting the Definition of Done.

**Sprint Goal:** A single objective for the Sprint. Created during Sprint Planning. Provides
coherence and focus. Even if the work evolves, the Sprint Goal does not change.

**Sprint Backlog:** Comprises the Sprint Goal, the set of selected Product Backlog Items,
and the actionable plan for delivering the Increment.

**BA preparation checklist:**
- All candidate PBIs meet the Definition of Ready
- Acceptance criteria for candidate PBIs are confirmed and unambiguous
- Dependencies affecting this Sprint are documented
- Sprint Goal draft is ready for Product Owner to propose

## Daily Scrum

**Purpose:** Inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessary.
**Attendees:** Developers (the event is for and by the Developers)
**Time-box:** 15 minutes, same time and place every day of the Sprint

**Key facts:**
- The Product Owner and Scrum Master do not facilitate it unless they are also Developers
- The Developers can select any structure for the event — the three questions format is not
  mandated by the Scrum Guide 2020
- If the Product Owner or Scrum Master are working on Sprint Backlog Items, they participate as Developers

**What it is NOT:**
- A status report to management
- A blocker escalation meeting
- A planning session

**BA implication:** The BA is not part of the Daily Scrum unless acting as a Developer.
The BA's contribution is maintaining the impediment and dependency log so the Developers
have what they need. The BA resolves BA-owned blockers quickly without waiting for the
next Daily Scrum.

## Sprint Review

**Purpose:** Inspect the outcome of the Sprint and determine future adaptations.
**Attendees:** Scrum Team and key stakeholders invited by the Product Owner
**Time-box:** 4 hours maximum for a one-month Sprint

**What happens:**
- The Scrum Team presents the results of their work to key stakeholders
- The group discusses what was Done and what changed in their environment
- Attendees collaborate on what to do next
- The Product Backlog may be adjusted to reflect new opportunities

**The Increment is presented:** Only work that meets the Definition of Done is presented as Done.
Unfinished work is not presented as delivered.

**Key distinction:** The Sprint Review is not a demo-only event — it is a collaborative working
session to adapt the Product Backlog and discuss the path forward.

**BA preparation checklist:**
- Acceptance evidence table complete for all committed stories
- Unfinished stories documented with reason and disposition (return to Product Backlog)
- Sprint output report ready for stakeholders who cannot attend
- Any new requirements or changes identified during the Sprint surfaced for Product Backlog consideration

## Sprint Retrospective

**Purpose:** Plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness.
**Attendees:** Scrum Team (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers)
**Time-box:** 3 hours maximum for a one-month Sprint

**What the Scrum Team inspects:**
- Individuals and interactions
- Processes and tools
- Definition of Done (with quality implications)

**Output required by Scrum Guide 2020:**
At least one improvement identified in the Retrospective is added to the Sprint Backlog
for the next Sprint. This is not optional.

**BA observation areas (contribute, do not dominate):**
- Requirements quality: were stories clear enough to avoid mid-Sprint BA interruptions?
- Acceptance criteria: were any criteria disputed or reinterpreted during the Sprint?
- Handoff friction: were BA → Developer handoffs smooth?
- Stakeholder availability: did unavailable stakeholders cause delays?

**What the Retrospective is NOT:**
- A blame session
- A requirements meeting
- A planning meeting for the next Sprint (that is Sprint Planning)

## Scrum Artefacts — BA Connections

### Product Backlog
**Owner:** Product Owner
**BA contribution:** User story writing, acceptance criteria, backlog refinement support
**Commitment:** Product Goal — the long-term objective the Product Backlog serves

### Sprint Backlog
**Owner:** Developers
**BA contribution:** DoR verification, acceptance criteria confirmation, impediment resolution
**Commitment:** Sprint Goal

### Increment
**Owner:** Scrum Team collectively
**BA contribution:** Acceptance evidence, DoD verification
**Commitment:** Definition of Done

## Scrum Guide 2020 — Key Terminology Reference

| Correct Term | Do Not Use |
|-------------|-----------|
| Sprint | Iteration, cycle, timebox (as a noun for the Sprint) |
| Scrum events | Ceremonies, meetings, sessions |
| Developers | Development team, dev team, engineering team |
| Product Backlog Item (PBI) | Ticket, card, story (story is acceptable informally) |
| Increment | Release candidate, build, deliverable |
| Sprint Backlog | Sprint tasks, task board (the board is a tool, not the artefact) |
| Product Owner | PO (acceptable abbreviation), not Business Analyst or Project Manager |
| Scrum Master | SM (acceptable abbreviation), not project manager, not facilitator as a title |
| Sprint Goal | Sprint objective (Sprint Goal is the Scrum Guide term) |
| Definition of Done | DoD (acceptable abbreviation), not acceptance criteria for the team |
| Definition of Ready | DoR (acceptable abbreviation) — not in the Scrum Guide 2020 but widely adopted |
